# Future Recommendations, Production Roadmap & Next Steps

**Purpose:** Turn the lore and 15-mission campaign into a manageable game-development plan without losing narrative consistency.

1\. Recommended order of work

Do not attempt to build all 15 missions at once. First prove that the core game is enjoyable and that the story can be delivered through playable missions.

**Recommended sequence:**

1.  Lock the canon and terminology.

2.  Prototype the flight controls and one space mission.

3.  Prototype ground exploration and one horror moment.

4.  Build a small carrier/hub and mission-to-mission flow.

5.  Test the first three missions as a vertical slice.

6.  Only then expand the mission count, enemy roster, cinematic sequences, and ancient environments.

2\. Resolve these canon questions before full production

These are deliberate decisions to lock, not requests to invent more lore immediately.

# A. The Heralds and the Hollow King

The campaign currently includes surviving Herald forces and the Hollow King's Empty Host. Decide clearly whether the Empty Host consists primarily of Heralds, other species, human defectors, or a mixed coalition. Recommended direction: a mixed force shaped by the King's long campaign, with Herald survivors among its senior members. This avoids making every Herald automatically an enemy.

# B. The Word and the Remnant

The lore identifies the Word as God and the Remnant as a faith, while leaving the actual metaphysical truth open. Preserve that ambiguity. Avoid confirming or denying the faith's theology too early; let characters interpret events differently.

# C. Zeal

Define its visible gameplay rules before designing many ancient puzzles. Recommended baseline:

- Zeal allows Michael to sense and interface with specific ancient systems.

- It does not grant unrestricted telekinesis, omniscience, or unlimited combat power.

- It may cause strain, expose Michael to dangerous feedback, or require environmental conditions.

- Its narrative meaning is responsibility, not destiny or moral perfection.

# D. The Spear of Destiny

Lock the distinction between its two uses:

- \*\*Historical use:\*\* a weaponized interface that helped defeat the Heralds and damaged the containment system.

- \*\*Final use:\*\* a carefully controlled interface that reinforces the barrier instead of forcing the system to submit.

This distinction must be communicated through gameplay and story, not only dialogue.

# E. The Drowned Watcher

Recommended canon:

- Native to Elyra's oceans; not created by the Hollow King.

- The King influences it through an ancient signal/interface.

- Its control is imperfect and can fail.

- The first game emphasizes foreshadowing and one major physical incident rather than a conventional boss battle.

- A later game can explore the creature's true anatomy, behavior, and what happens when the control signal is severed.

# F. Timeline and geography

Before writing dialogue, create a one-page timeline for:

- The first warning.

- Herald arrival on Elyra.

- The Great Collapse.

- Humanity's use of the Spear.

- The founding of the Keepers and rise of the Remnant.

- Humanity's discovery and settlement of Astraea.

- The first reactivation of ancient machinery.

- The Hollow King's formation of the Empty Host.

- The events of the 15-mission campaign.

Also create a simple location map showing Elyra, the major human orbital facilities, the remote settlement, the oceanic monitoring site, Astraea, the ruined city, the Testament, and the Mountain. Do not add locations merely to make the map look larger; each location should serve a story or gameplay purpose.

3\. Vertical-slice prototypes

# Prototype A — Mission 1: First Watch

**Goal:** Prove that space flight feels good and that the opening mystery can work without long exposition.

Build:

- Interceptor movement, turning, acceleration, and braking.

- Flight camera and basic HUD.

- Target selection, target lock, and weapon firing.

- Basic enemy fighter AI.

- Carrier launch and return.

- A damaged transport to inspect.

- A short dogfight with clear objectives.

- Flight recorder recovery.

- One brief sensor event showing lights beneath Elyra's ocean.

- Debrief screen with the unexplained signal.

Success criteria:

- The player understands flight controls without a long tutorial.

- Combat is readable and satisfying.

- The inspection section creates curiosity.

- The Drowned Watcher clue is noticeable but not explained.

# Prototype B — Mission 2: The Quiet Settlement

**Goal:** Prove that ground exploration can create tension without constant combat.

Build:

- Third-person movement and camera.

- Interaction and scanning.

- A small settlement with a few explorable buildings.

- Clues that can be found in more than one order.

- Basic squad follow/hold behavior.

- Short dialogue triggers.

- One restrained combat encounter.

- One unreliable-radio horror moment.

- An extraction objective.

Success criteria:

- The player naturally looks for evidence.

- Environmental storytelling is understandable without a lengthy briefing.

- Horror comes from uncertainty and sound.

- The mission does not feel empty or like a walking simulator.

# Prototype C — Mission 3: Orders from Veyr

**Goal:** Prove the structure connecting a mission, a hub, a character scene, and the next mission.

Build:

- A simple carrier interior or mission hub.

- Mission briefing and debriefing.

- Elias dialogue interaction.

- A screen or location for reviewing recovered evidence.

- Transport escort gameplay.

- A fighter ambush.

- A mission-completion transition.

Success criteria:

- The player understands how the campaign progresses.

- Elias feels important, not just like a mission announcer.

- The mystery and the military objectives feel connected.

4\. Production roadmap

# Phase 1 — Pre-production

Deliverables:

- Master lore bible.

- Campaign and mission design document.

- Character profiles.

- Faction and enemy roster.

- Timeline and location map.

- Visual reference boards for Elyra, Astraea, the Heralds, the Empty Host, the Mountain, and the Drowned Watcher.

- Core gameplay specification for flight, ground combat, interaction, scanning, and Zeal.

Exit condition: the major story facts and the first three mission objectives no longer contradict one another.

# Phase 2 — Core systems

Build only the systems required by the vertical slice:

- Flight controller and camera.

- Weapons and targeting.

- Basic enemy AI.

- Ground controller and interaction.

- Scanning and evidence collection.

- Mission objectives and checkpoints.

- Dialogue triggers and subtitles.

- Save/load and mission completion.

- Simple UI and audio events.

Exit condition: all core systems work in small test scenes before full environments are built.

# Phase 3 — Vertical slice

Build Missions 1–3 to a consistent level of quality. Use placeholder art where necessary, but include representative sound, lighting, objectives, and story delivery.

Exit condition: an external tester can play the slice and explain the protagonist's immediate goal, the mystery introduced, and why Elias matters.

# Phase 4 — Campaign production

Expand in order, keeping the story's revelation pacing:

- First, missions 4–6 and the ancient/horror systems.

- Then missions 7–9 and the large battle/Astraea systems.

- Then missions 10–12 and the Empty Host/Watcher/King reveals.

- Finally missions 13–15 and the Mountain/Spear finale.

Reuse mechanics intelligently, but make each mission's objective and rhythm distinct. Reuse should create consistency, not repetition.

# Phase 5 — Polish and testing

Test for:

- Clear objectives and readable combat.

- Flight handling and difficulty.

- Squad behavior and dialogue timing.

- Horror pacing and audio clarity.

- Lore consistency and terminology.

- Whether discoveries are understandable without excessive exposition.

- Whether the ending resolves Michael's arc while preserving the larger mystery.

- Performance in large battles and complex ancient environments.

5\. Design recommendations

# Keep space combat central

The premise promises a military sci-fi campaign, so flight cannot be a small side activity. Make the Interceptor a meaningful part of Michael's identity. Vary objectives: patrol, escort, reconnaissance, interception, defense, rescue, and escape.

# Make ground missions mechanically distinct

Avoid making every ground mission “move forward and shoot.” Give the player a reason to investigate, defend, extract, repair, infiltrate, or survive. Keep squad behavior simple and reliable before attempting complex squad tactics.

# Use the Drowned Watcher sparingly

The Watcher is strongest when the player sees evidence before seeing the creature. Do not reveal a clean full-body model in its first appearance. Use scale cues—lights, displaced water, damaged structures, sonar failure, and characters' reactions—to sell its size.

# Keep the Hollow mysterious

Show consequences and fragments, not a full encyclopedia entry. The first game needs a meaningful answer to the current conflict, not a definitive explanation of the Hollow's origin.

# Make the Spear's final use playable

The finale will be more satisfying if Michael must complete a sequence of actions that embody restraint and preservation: protect evacuation routes, stabilize the system, make a deliberate interface choice, and defend the activation. Avoid solving the crisis with a single cutscene button press.

# Give Elias meaningful agency

Elias should not only explain the lore. His earlier secrecy should have consequences, and his later actions should help Michael succeed. He should make at least one consequential choice that costs him something or changes the outcome.

# Avoid overloading every mission with revelations

Some missions should establish place, character, and atmosphere. If every level contains a major twist, the player will stop absorbing the twists. Let the quiet missions breathe.

- Military radio chatter and command calls.

- Interceptor engines, weapons, impacts, and targeting tones.

- Watcher audio cues that suggest scale without clearly identifying its body.

- Ancient machinery, signal pulses, and archive recordings.

- Distinct sound language for Zeal and the Spear.

- Music that shifts gradually from military confidence to uncertainty and cosmic dread.

7\. Risks to watch

| **Risk**                           | **Why it hurts the game**                           | **Mitigation**                                           |
|------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------|
| Too much lore too early            | Player stops absorbing revelations                  | Reveal information through objectives and discoveries    |
| Flight is underdeveloped           | Game fails to deliver on its military-pilot premise | Prototype flight before building many missions           |
| Ground missions feel repetitive    | Campaign becomes a sequence of generic firefights   | Give each mission a distinct verb and objective          |
| Watcher is overexposed             | Creature becomes an ordinary boss                   | Use rare appearances and incomplete views                |
| Hollow is overexplained            | Cosmic horror loses mystery                         | Show effects; reserve origin questions                   |
| King is treated as wholly right    | His atrocities lose moral weight                    | Separate correct diagnosis from unjust solution          |
| Spear solves everything too easily | Finale feels unearned                               | Require multiple gameplay objectives and meaningful risk |
| Keepers are all villains           | Moral conflict becomes simplistic                   | Give them sincere motives and different responses        |
| Timeline contradictions emerge     | Players lose trust in the story                     | Maintain a single timeline and terminology list          |

10\. Practical next action

The most useful next step is not to write more lore. It is to build and test a small, playable version of **First Watch**. That prototype will answer the biggest production question: whether the flight controls, mission objectives, and quiet mystery can work together. Once that foundation is enjoyable, the rest of the campaign can be built with much less guesswork.
