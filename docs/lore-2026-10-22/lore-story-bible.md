READ THIS UPDATE FIRST. The original Version 3.0 document follows unchanged for reference.

## CONTINUITY STATUS AND PRECEDENCE

This October 22, 2026 continuity update records decisions approved in discussion after Version 3.0. Read this update before the preserved Version 3.0 text below. Where the original text conflicts, this update takes precedence. Unresolved items are placeholders, not finalized lore. The original material remains intact for development reference; it has not been silently rewritten.

## MASTER LORE — CURRENT INTERPRETATION

Earth is humanity’s lost original home; Elyra is the home of the post-Collapse generations. The Great Collapse around 2154 and Elyra arrival around 2157 remain proposed historical anchors from Version 3.0. The First Civilization predates the Heralds and left ruins and some intact advanced structures or systems. Whether it originated on Elyra is not yet fixed. The Hollow’s balancing role is metaphysical, while the suppressor’s protective dome is technology; neither means evil disappears or choices stop mattering.

## NARRATIVE GUARDRAILS

The Hollow King may understand suppression technically yet reject its moral conclusion; corruption should not turn him into an unintelligent puppet. The Heralds must not be reduced to uniformly evil invaders. Elias must not be written as a conspirator. The Drowned Watcher remains a separate intelligent aquatic being.

## APPROVED UNIVERSAL RULES

The Hollow is a real, necessary, nonphysical universal balancing force. It cannot be killed or eliminated; its destructive influence can be resisted, managed, and greatly suppressed. It is not the Drowned Watcher or a conventional physical boss. The Hollow King recognizes the danger of the Hollow but is fully consumed by its influence. He understands suppression technology and rejects its implications. His fate in the existing game is defeat, not a confirmed death.

## SUPPRESSION TECHNOLOGY

The First Civilization invented the Hollow suppressor; the Heralds recovered and adapted it. Pathfinder steals a suppressor from the Empty Host moon installation on March 8, 2237. The device can project a protective dome of variable size, up to roughly a city (not a country). It requires no external power supply and massively reduces Hollow influence rather than granting immunity or destroying the Hollow. Working explanation, not yet a precisely defined mechanism: its protective strength may be sustained by the people inside actively resisting corruption, and may falter when that resistance collapses.

## HERALDS AND TIDE CITY

The Heralds were originally peaceful and possessed valuable knowledge, including the ancient suppression technology. Hollow corruption and a civil war fractured them. Their attack on Tide City is explained by the combination of civil war, Hollow influence, and misunderstanding; this is the agreed narrative direction, with detailed causes and participants still to be dramatized. Add more distinct Herald ships in the final battle, including ships that can function as combat distractions without implying every Herald is hostile.

## CHARACTERS AND REVELATIONS

Elias Veyr is a genuinely good, ordinary senior Keeper and mentor. He does not secretly possess the truth about Earth or the Hollow, does not intentionally conceal it from Michael, and has no supernatural powers. The Hollow King alone knows the deeper truth about Earth and the Hollow; surviving public histories or records do not automatically convey that full truth. Michael has foreshadowing dreams caused by a combination of Hollow influence and the Drowned Watcher’s mind-to-mind communication, plausibly aided by Elyra’s extensive water. Dreams should feature the Watcher and significant figures, not imply Michael visited Astraea, and should lead toward revelations rather than deliver explanations. Some dreams can become cinematic cutscenes.

## ENDING RULES

In the existing destructive Tide City route, the Spear kills everyone in the atmosphere/area affected by its discharge; Tide City is physically saved but humanity suffers a profound moral failure. Do not silently weaken this rule. The secret true ending is not literal killing of the Hollow: it is resisting/suppressing its corruption, avoiding slaughter of the Heralds, and making coexistence and possible peace achievable. Exact gameplay triggers and the reach of the Spear require alignment with the playable ending; do not replace them with invented conditions.

## PRESERVED MYSTERIES

Michael’s name appears carved beneath the Hollow King’s mountain with the date March 16, 2237. This is intentional and remains unexplained. The Pathfinder crew’s inscription bears March 8, 2237. The Drowned Watcher’s independence, the three recovered-footage reels, the ELYRA AI’s statement that she did not transport Michael’s team to the mountain, the menu secret “The Call,” and the phrase “Second Collapse” remain mysteries or unresolved continuity elements, not licenses to invent explanations.

## PATHFINDER-TO-KEEPERS TRANSFER

Pathfinder S3 ends March 10 with the Empty Host capturing the surviving crew and retaking the suppressor. Between March 11 and March 15, a survivor escapes or is freed from the Hollow King’s mountain, obtains the suppressor, and delivers it to the Keepers. This is an agreed continuity bridge; the survivor’s identity, means of escape, device recovery, and precise delivery scene/date remain open pending Pathfinder S3 and Archive cutscenes.

## PRODUCTION / CAMPAIGN RULE

Existing playable content has priority over the older speculative 15-mission outline: the prologue and Archives 01–03, Pathfinder S1, and existing footage/endings are not to be silently replaced. The goal remains a flexible 15-main-mission campaign, separate from Pathfinder’s three-mission side arc; earlier missions should have their own smaller Archives and escalating revelations. Next priority is Pathfinder S2. No new mission numbering or additional playable levels are approved by this update.

# ORIGINAL VERSION 3.0 — PRESERVED SOURCE TEXT

**ELYRA: THE HOLLOW**

# **DOCUMENT 1 — MASTER LORE & STORY BIBLE**

**Version:** 3.0 — October 21, 2026  
**Genre:** Military science fiction, space combat, ground operations, mystery, religious science fiction, cosmic horror  
**Format:** Linear, story-driven single-player campaign  
**Primary protagonist:** Lieutenant Michael Hall  
**Primary antagonist:** The Hollow King  
**Central themes:** Creation, balance, free will, corruption, responsibility, faith, and the consequences of power

## **1. CORE VISION**

Elyra: The Hollow is a cinematic military science-fiction game set 83 years after humanity lost Earth during a catastrophe known as the Great Collapse.

Humanity survived aboard nine evacuation arks, only three of which reached the oceanic world of Elyra.

The survivors rebuilt their civilization under the protection of the Keepers, a military and religious order dedicated to preserving humanity's knowledge and preventing another catastrophe.

The Keepers believe the Great Collapse was a war against an alien civilization known as the Heralds.

Their understanding is incomplete.

The Heralds were themselves fleeing a catastrophe that had devastated their homeworld, Astraea.

That catastrophe was connected to a universal force known as the Hollow.

The Hollow is not a monster, army, or physical being. It is a necessary counterforce within creation, preventing intelligent civilizations from advancing without limit.

As civilizations approach dangerous levels of power, the Hollow's influence intensifies.

It calls to individuals, magnifies their ambitions and fears, and gradually turns civilizations against themselves.

The Hollow King has discovered part of this truth.

He believes that humanity must be controlled or eliminated before it threatens creation.

He is correct that humanity presents a danger.

He is wrong to assume extermination is the only possible solution.

Michael Hall must discover whether humanity can survive the Hollow's influence without destroying the universal balance it maintains.

## **2. THE COSMOLOGY OF CREATION**

### **2.1 The Maker and the Word**

The Remnant teaches that existence was intentionally created by the Maker.

The Word represents the divine act through which creation was brought into existence and given order.

These beliefs are central to the Remnant's religious identity.

Whether the Remnant's theological interpretation is entirely correct remains unknown.

The story must distinguish between what characters believe, what ancient records claim, and what the universe objectively demonstrates.

### **2.2 The Hollow**

The Hollow is a universal force that exists throughout creation.

Its fundamental function is balance.

Without limitation, sufficiently advanced civilizations could eventually obtain the ability to alter planets, stars, dimensions, or the underlying structure of reality.

The Hollow prevents unchecked advancement from continuing indefinitely.

It does not necessarily act through a conscious plan comparable to human decision-making.

Its influence manifests through intelligent beings.

It calls to their ambitions, fears, convictions, and desires.

The greater a civilization's power becomes, the more dangerous this influence can become.

### **2.3 The Cycle of Civilizations**

The observed cycle follows a recurring pattern:

- A civilization develops intelligence, cooperation, and increasingly advanced technology.

- It expands beyond its original world.

- Its achievements begin affecting larger systems.

- The Hollow's influence intensifies.

- Individuals become increasingly obsessed with power, security, control, or ideological purity.

- Internal divisions escalate.

- Civil war, technological catastrophe, or systemic collapse follows.

- The civilization's power diminishes, restoring a form of universal balance.

This cycle is real, but the precise threshold that activates stronger Hollow influence remains unknown.

Not every civilization necessarily collapses in the same way.

### **2.4 The Hollow's Corruption**

The Hollow does not simply turn people into mindless monsters.

Its influence can be subtle.

An affected individual may remain intelligent, compassionate toward selected people, and capable of logical reasoning.

However, their judgment becomes distorted.

They may increasingly believe that cruelty is necessary, that disagreement is treason, or that only they possess the truth.

Corruption can be resisted.

The Hollow's influence does not automatically remove moral responsibility.

### **2.5 Physical Monsters**

The Hollow itself never becomes a physical creature.

However, intelligent and nonintelligent organisms can exist within its influence.

Some may become violent or altered.

Others may retain their intelligence and independent judgment.

Physical creatures encountered in the game must not automatically be identified as the Hollow itself.

## **3. THE FIRST CIVILIZATION**

Long before the Heralds, an unidentified advanced civilization existed across multiple worlds.

Its surviving structures display extraordinary technological capabilities.

These include seamless white architecture, machinery that responds to unusual forms of consciousness, and systems that manipulate space in ways modern civilizations cannot explain.

The First Civilization encountered the Hollow's influence.

Unlike many civilizations before it, it discovered a method of resisting the destructive cycle.

Its achievement did not necessarily involve destroying the Hollow.

Instead, it appears to have developed a way to survive and advance while preserving the balance of creation.

This distinction is fundamental.

If the Hollow is necessary, eliminating it could be as dangerous as surrendering to its corruption.

The First Civilization may have discovered a third path.

Its ultimate fate remains unknown.

Its surviving installations are scattered across ancient worlds, including Astraea.

The Pathfinder expedition becomes the first modern human mission to uncover substantial evidence of this ancient resistance.

### **The White Structures**

White stone architecture appears on both Astraea and Elyra.

The structures appear to predate the civilizations currently inhabiting those worlds.

Their common appearance suggests a possible connection.

That connection is not yet proven.

The white city on Elyra and the white structure on Astraea should remain geographically distinct.

## **4. EARTH AND THE GREAT COLLAPSE**

### **4.1 Earth Before the Collapse**

Earth was humanity's original homeworld.

By the middle of the 22nd century, humanity had developed advanced military, industrial, and spacefaring technology.

Its expanding capabilities brought it into contact with the Heralds.

### **4.2 The Herald Warning**

The Heralds arrived during Earth's final years.

Their appearance was interpreted differently by different human groups.

Some believed they were divine messengers.

Others believed they represented an invading force.

The Heralds carried a warning:

"Something is coming."

Humanity did not fully understand the warning.

The resulting conflict became remembered as the First Contact War.

### **4.3 The Spear of Destiny**

During the war, humanity developed the Spear of Destiny, also called the Holy Lance.

It combined human engineering with captured Herald technology.

The Spear became humanity's greatest military achievement.

Its activation helped humanity resist the Heralds.

However, it also damaged an ancient containment system associated with the Hollow.

The exact relationship between the Spear's activation and Earth's final destruction remains an important historical mystery.

### **4.4 Earth's Destruction**

**Proposed official date: 2154.**

Earth was lost during the Great Collapse.

Nine evacuation arks departed.

Only three reached Elyra.

The survivors carried military equipment, historical records, religious traditions, and the Spear of Destiny.

### **4.5 The Consequences of Recent History**

The Great Collapse occurred only 83 years before the main campaign.

This means some elderly survivors may still remember Earth firsthand.

Humanity's incomplete history cannot be explained solely through the passage of centuries.

Records were lost during evacuation, but some were also classified, selectively preserved, or concealed.

The Keepers' historical authority therefore becomes an important source of conflict.

## **5. ELYRA — HUMANITY'S SECOND HOME**

Elyra is an ocean-dominated world with island chains, coastal settlements, maritime industry, floating platforms, deep trenches, and ancient submerged structures.

The surviving arks established humanity's new civilization here.

### **Tide City**

Tide City is humanity's central settlement and the principal seat of Keeper authority.

It was built near a preexisting white stone city.

The Spear of Destiny is preserved beneath the modern settlement.

Tide City represents both humanity's survival and its unresolved relationship with the past.

### **The Southern Mountains**

The Hollow King maintains a stronghold within Elyra's southern mountains.

The Keepers sometimes refer to this stronghold as "the Hollow."

This is a geographical nickname and must not be confused with the universal force.

Beneath the mountain lies a vast underground lake connected to Elyra's oceans.

The Drowned Watcher can travel through this aquatic system.

## **6. ASTRAEA — THE HERALD HOMEWORLD**

Astraea is the ancestral homeworld of the Heralds.

It contains ancient cities, ruined industrial regions, devastated deserts, surviving ecosystems, and installations predating Herald civilization.

The entire planet is not a wasteland.

Its dead red desert is one region damaged by the ongoing conflict.

Astraea also contains ancient structures associated with the First Civilization.

### **The Herald Civil War**

By March 2237, the Heralds are divided.

Some seek to preserve ancient systems.

Others seek to destroy them.

Others are attempting to escape the planet.

Their conflict has been intensified by the Hollow's influence.

The Pathfinder witnesses the civil war without immediately understanding its cause.

### **The Dead Moon**

Astraea possesses a dead moon containing an Empty Host installation.

The Hollow King's forces recovered an ancient white stone object and brought it to this installation.

Captain Rhen's team steals the object on March 8, 2237.

## **7. THE REMNANT**

The Remnant is the faith and collective identity of humanity's survivors.

Its teachings combine older human religious traditions with the experience of the Great Collapse.

The Remnant teaches that creation has purpose and that humanity has a responsibility to preserve life.

Its central moral themes include restraint, sacrifice, compassion, and responsibility.

These teachings may help individuals resist the Hollow's corrupting influence.

However, the faith's institutions are not automatically immune to corruption.

Religious certainty can become fanaticism when separated from humility and moral judgment.

## **8. THE KEEPERS**

The Keepers are humanity's military and religious order.

They operate the fleet, train Interceptors, preserve historical records, and defend human settlements.

They guard the Archive and its central historical and navigational record.

Their traditions may provide practical resistance to the Hollow's influence.

This resistance is not immunity.

A Keeper can still become corrupted through fear, ambition, pride, or unquestioning obedience.

The order's central institutional weakness is its willingness to conceal dangerous truths in the name of stability.

## **9. THE CODEX**

The Codex is the Keepers' administrative name for the Testament of the First Word.

It contains essential historical and navigational knowledge, including routes between humanity's colonies.

On March 5, 2237, the Hollow King steals it.

### **Revised Motivation**

The Hollow King believes unrestricted access to ancient worlds and technologies threatens creation.

By controlling the Codex, he can restrict humanity's movement and prevent the discovery or activation of dangerous systems.

He also gains strategic control over routes necessary for humanity's survival.

The Codex may contain routes to sites associated with the First Civilization.

The King may therefore be preventing humanity from discovering an alternative to his own proposed solution.

This deeper connection remains a planned revelation rather than confirmed historical fact.

## **10. THE HOLLOW KING**

The Hollow King is a powerful, intelligent antagonist who understands aspects of the universal cycle.

He has witnessed or studied the destruction of advanced civilizations.

He believes that humanity is approaching the same fate.

His objective is to prevent another catastrophe.

His chosen methods are domination, military conquest, and potentially extermination.

The Hollow has influenced his judgment.

However, he remains responsible for his decisions.

His tragedy is that he may correctly identify the danger while becoming an instrument of the same destructive process.

He believes control is necessary.

Michael must demonstrate that responsibility is different from control.

## **11. THE EMPTY HOST**

The Empty Host is the Hollow King's military force.

Its soldiers are disciplined, relentless, and often silent.

They target military installations, ancient structures, navigational systems, and containment machinery.

The precise species composition of the Empty Host remains unresolved.

A mixed force of different species, including corrupted or coerced individuals, is a recommended direction.

The Empty Host must remain distinct from the Herald civilization as a whole.

Not every Herald is an enemy.

## **12. THE DROWNED WATCHER**

The Drowned Watcher is a massive, intelligent aquatic organism native to Elyra.

It is neither inherently good nor evil.

It possesses independent judgment.

The Hollow King can influence its behavior through ancient technology, but his control is imperfect.

The Watcher has repeatedly attempted to warn Michael against activating the Spear.

It takes the Codex into the underground lake during Archive 01.

It also refuses the King's attempt to feed the captured Pathfinder crew to it.

Its behavior suggests that it recognizes a danger greater than the immediate conflict.

The Watcher is not the Hollow.

Its full motives remain mysterious.

## **13. ZEAL**

Zeal is an unusual ability or sensitivity possessed by Michael.

It allows him to interact with certain ancient systems and perceive phenomena beyond ordinary human senses.

Zeal may also help him resist the Hollow's influence.

Its origins remain unknown.

It must not provide unlimited power or immunity from corruption.

Its central narrative meaning is disciplined responsibility rather than unquestioned destiny.

## **14. THE PATHFINDER ARTIFACT**

The Pathfinder recovers an unnamed object from the Empty Host's dead-moon installation.

The object is made from seamless white material.

It is neither human nor Empty Host technology.

It appears related to ancient structures on Astraea.

During the crash, it shows Landry an image or impression of a buried structure.

The new Pathfinder storyline connects it to evidence that the First Civilization resisted the Hollow.

Its precise function remains undecided.

Possible functions include a key, archive interface, navigational device, or component of a larger system.

The object should remain unnamed until its identity becomes a meaningful story revelation.

## **15. THE ANCIENT PLANET-SCALE VESSEL**

A future storyline may introduce an enormous ancient vessel capable of threatening an entire planet.

The vessel may originate from the First Civilization.

Its original purpose is unknown.

It may have been designed for containment, evacuation, or extreme intervention during civilization-wide catastrophes.

It should not be established immediately as a simple planet-destroying weapon.

Its existence can be foreshadowed through ancient records and impossible-scale machinery.

Its full activation is reserved for a later major revelation.

## **16. THE CENTRAL MORAL CONFLICT**

The Hollow King believes:

"Creation must be protected from civilizations that cannot control themselves."

Michael eventually recognizes:

"Power creates responsibility, but responsibility does not require extermination."

The Hollow is necessary.

Its corrupting influence is real.

The cycle of destruction is real.

But the First Civilization's surviving knowledge suggests that destruction may not be the only possible outcome.

The game's central question is:

**Can intelligent life become powerful enough to understand creation without becoming so arrogant that it destroys itself?**

## **17. MYSTERIES TO PRESERVE**

- The exact origin of the Hollow.

- Why the Hollow is necessary to universal balance.

- The precise threshold at which its influence intensifies.

- How the First Civilization resisted its destructive influence.

- Why the First Civilization disappeared.

- Whether the ancient white structures share a common origin.

- The true purpose of the Pathfinder artifact.

- The full relationship between Zeal and ancient technology.

- What the Drowned Watcher understands about the Spear.

- Why Michael's name appears beneath the mountain with a future date.

- What caused the Great Collapse beyond the official explanation.

- The original purpose of the ancient planet-scale vessel.

The first game should resolve its immediate human conflict without explaining every cosmic mystery.

## **18. CANON RULES**

- Earth is humanity's original homeworld.

- Elyra is humanity's adopted homeworld.

- Astraea is the Herald homeworld.

- The Great Collapse occurred approximately 83 years before March 2237, with 2154 proposed as its date.

- The Hollow is a necessary universal balancing force.

- The Hollow is not a physical monster.

- Intelligent beings can resist the Hollow's corrupting influence.

- The Hollow King understands a genuine danger but has adopted destructive methods.

- The Drowned Watcher is an independent, sentient native creature.

- The First Civilization discovered a method of resistance.

- The Pathfinder uncovers evidence of that method.

- The Spear remains dangerous, and its activation must have consequences.

- The older campaign's Astraea climax must not silently replace the existing Tide City climax.

- Unresolved mysteries must remain mysteries until deliberately revealed.

- The story must distinguish established facts, character beliefs, and misleading historical accounts.
